# First Light privacy policy

_Last updated: October 27, 2026_

First Light is a browser extension that replaces your new tab page with a clock, live telescope observations, a background picture, a to-do list and, if you connect one, your Google Calendar events for the day.

## What the extension stores

Everything the extension stores stays in your browser's local storage on your device, in the extension's own storage area:

- your settings, including any Google Calendar addresses you paste in
- your to-do items
- cached copies of the telescope data, background picture details and today's calendar events, so a new tab opens instantly

None of this is sent to the developer or to anyone else. There are no accounts, analytics, ads or tracking. Removing the extension deletes all of it.

## What the extension downloads

To show its content, the extension makes requests directly from your browser to these services. It sends them only what is needed to fetch the data, never your to-dos or settings.

| Service | Why |
| --- | --- |
| spacetelescopelive.org | What the JWST and Hubble space telescopes are observing |
| usdf-rsp.slac.stanford.edu | Rubin Observatory's recent survey visits |
| science.nasa.gov | NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day |
| esawebb.org, esahubble.org | Images for the shuffle background |
| calendar.google.com | Your calendar events, only if you connect a calendar |

As with any website, these services can see your IP address and the time of each request. Their own privacy policies apply to that.

## Google Calendar

The calendar feature is optional and off until you paste in a calendar's "Secret address in iCal format". The extension asks for permission to access calendar.google.com at that point, and you can revoke it at any time from your browser's extension settings. Events are read only to show today's schedule on your new tab page. They are cached on your device and are never sent anywhere else. The extension cannot create, change or delete events.

The secret address gives read access to that calendar, so don't share it. Anyone using the same browser profile can see it in the extension's settings. If you think it has been exposed, reset it in Google Calendar's settings.
